Executive Summary

  1. Original TextureSAM on RWTD fails mainly by fragmentation, not pure coverage.
  2. First improvement was TextureSAM-v2 consolidation (graph merge + global single-mask objective).
  3. DTD-trained descriptor branch was added and tested; it improved IoU but underperformed handcrafted on ARI.
  4. Largest leap came from a new multi-bank reranker over v2/v3/v4/v5 prompt mask banks.
  5. Edge-aware refinement (GrabCut seeded by selected mask) gave additional gains on both mIoU and ARI.
  6. For robustness: use CV+Refine as the primary deployable method; avoid in-sample as final claim metric.

What Changed vs Original TextureSAM (Step-by-Step, Easy Version)

Step 1: Keep high-recall SAM proposals

Instead of suppressing many masks, keep rich fragmented proposals to preserve coverage.

Step 2: Build texture descriptors per mask

Two modes were implemented: handcrafted filter-bank descriptors and a DTD-trained small CNN embedding.

Step 3: Merge texture-consistent neighbors

Construct a proposal graph, score edges by texture similarity + weak boundary + low heterogeneity, then union-find merge.

Step 4: Enforce one coherent binary region

Select final component by S(C)=Delta(in,out)-lambda*Var(C)-mu*Frag(C) and postprocess holes/components.

Step 5: Scale candidate diversity with v2/v3/v4/v5 prompt banks

Pool proposals from four upstream runs, plus per-run union masks, to increase candidate quality per image.

Step 6: Learn a quality reranker

Train RandomForest to predict candidate IoU from texture/shape/consensus features; pick top candidate.

Step 7: Add edge-aware refinement

Run GrabCut with selected mask as seed and keep largest component for cleaner boundaries.

Step 8: Verify with tests and ablations

Unit tests passed (5/5), descriptor modes compared, CV and in-sample modes both measured.

Robustness Question: Best Method for Any Texture Image?

Recommendation: For practical deployment, the best default is TextureSAM-2 Reranker (CV + edge-aware refine).

It is the strongest method with held-out evaluation and avoids the optimism of in-sample fitting.
